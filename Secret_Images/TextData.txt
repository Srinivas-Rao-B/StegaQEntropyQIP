In 2026, the StegaQEntropy research team conducted a controlled experiment on secure image communication using DWT-based embedding, entropy intelligence, machine-learning region selection, QRNG-derived randomness, and simulated QKD. The study examined how payload size, image texture, local variance, entropy, gradient strength, noise characteristics, and regional capacity affect imperceptibility and reliable extraction. Each experiment used a structured binary payload containing ordinary words, technical terms, numbers, uppercase and lowercase identifiers, and special characters so that the embedding system was tested with realistic and diverse data. The researchers evaluated BER, QBER, MSE, PSNR, recovered length, embedding operations, and execution time for every trial. The objective was to recover the complete intended payload without bit errors while keeping the visual difference between the cover image and stego image extremely small. Randomized region selection and coefficient ordering were controlled through the established randomness and key-generation workflow, while the adaptive embedding strength was adjusted according to regional characteristics and coefficient suitability. The methodology also tracked the relationship between payload demand and available coefficient capacity rather than assuming that every image could safely carry the same amount of information. During evaluation, the system compared multiple payload configurations and recorded whether the selected regions could accommodate the message without excessive distortion. The experiment included values such as 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, and 8192, together with identifiers such as SRI, DWT, ML, QKD, QRNG, and GA. It also included mixed-case words such as Secure, Adaptive, Reliable, Research, Analysis, and Communication, along with symbols, ! @ # $ % & * ( ) - _ + = ? / : ; and punctuation. The final analysis focused on determining a practical operating point where payload capacity, extraction accuracy, randomness, regional intelligence, and image quality remained balanced. The results were intended to provide a reproducible basis for evaluating a hybrid steganographic architecture under more demanding payload conditions.
